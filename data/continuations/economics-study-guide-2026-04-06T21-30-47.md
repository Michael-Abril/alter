# **MACROECONOMICS STUDY GUIDE**
*GDP, CPI, Inflation & Economic Growth Essentials*

## **SECTION 1: MEASURING THE ECONOMY**

### **GDP Fundamentals**
**Expenditure Approach:** `Y = C + I + G + NX`
- **C** (Consumption): Household spending + imputed rent for owner-occupied homes
- **I** (Investment): Business capital + new housing + inventory changes
- **G** (Government): Public spending (excludes transfers like Social Security)
- **NX** (Net Exports): Exports - Imports

**GDP vs GNP:** GDP measures production within borders; GNP measures production by citizens

**GDP Traps to Avoid:**
- Don't double-count intermediate goods
- Include only final goods and services
- Government transfers ≠ government purchases
- Used goods sales don't count (already counted when new)

### **Real vs Nominal Calculations**
- **GDP Deflator** = `(Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100`
- **Real GDP** = `(Nominal GDP ÷ GDP Deflator) × 100`
- **GDP Deflator vs CPI:** Deflator uses current basket, CPI uses fixed basket

## **SECTION 2: CONSUMER PRICE INDEX (CPI)**

### **5-Step CPI Process:**
1. **Fix the basket** of goods/services
2. **Find prices** for each period
3. **Compute basket cost** for each period
4. **Choose base year** and set CPI = 100
5. **Calculate CPI** = `(Current Cost ÷ Base Year Cost) × 100`

**Inflation Rate** = `[(CPI₂ - CPI₁) ÷ CPI₁] × 100`

**Converting Dollars Over Time:**
`Value in Year B = Amount in Year A × (CPI_B ÷ CPI_A)`

### **CPI Limitations:**
- **Substitution bias:** Fixed basket ignores consumer substitution
- **New goods:** Doesn't capture quality improvements
- **Outlet bias:** Doesn't account for discount retailers

## **SECTION 3: LABOR MARKET**

### **Key Formulas:**
- **Labor Force** = Employed + Unemployed
- **Unemployment Rate** = `(Unemployed ÷ Labor Force) × 100`
- **Labor Force Participation** = `(Labor Force ÷ Adult Population) × 100`

**Discouraged Worker Trap:** Not unemployed if stopped looking for work (not in labor force)

**Types of Unemployment:**
- **Frictional:** Job searching/transitions
- **Structural:** Skills mismatch/technological change  
- **Cyclical:** Economic downturns
- **Natural Rate** = Frictional + Structural

## **SECTION 4: PRODUCTION & GROWTH**

### **Production Function**
`Y = A × F(L, K, H, N)`
- **Y**: Output  
- **A**: Technology/Total Factor Productivity
- **L**: Labor  
- **K**: Physical Capital
- **H**: Human Capital  
- **N**: Natural Resources

### **Growth Accounting:**
**Growth in Output = Growth in A + α × Growth in K + (1-α) × Growth in L**
- α ≈ 0.3 (capital's share)

**Catch-up Effect:** Poor countries grow faster than rich countries (diminishing returns to capital)

## **SECTION 5: MONETARY POLICY**

### **The 3 Policy Shocks ("The Exam Shifts"):**
1. **Demand Shocks:** Change aggregate demand (fiscal/monetary policy)
2. **Supply Shocks:** Change production costs (oil prices, technology)
3. **Policy Regime Changes:** New rules/institutions

### **Financial System:**
**Think of it as plumbing:** Channels savings to investment

**Bonds vs Stocks:**
- **Bonds:** IOU/Debt, fixed payments, safer
- **Stocks:** Ownership claims, variable returns, riskier
- **Municipal bonds:** Tax-free (local gov't)
- **Corporate bonds:** Taxable (companies)

### **Money Supply Tools:**
1. **Open Market Operations:** Buy/sell government bonds
2. **Reserve Requirements:** Banks' required reserves
3. **Discount Rate:** Fed's lending rate to banks

**Money Multiplier** = `1 ÷ Reserve Ratio`
